# Special rules

¶ Advantage Rule

¶ When to Apply It

If a referee observes an infringement but stopping play would be detrimental to the infringed team, the referee allows play to continue. This is "playing advantage."

Think of it as: "Would stopping play actually help the team that was fouled, or would it take away an opportunity they already have?"

Advantage is played until the infringed team has reached an equivalent playing situation to what they would have had without the infringement.

¶ Losing the Advantage

If the advantage is lost before that equivalent situation is reached, the referee may award a delayed penalty at the location of the original infringement.

¶ Major Infringement + Advantage

When a major infringement occurs but advantage is being played:

The referee may still send the infringing player to the penalty box at the next stop in play.

Play restarts according to that stop (not from the location of the delayed penalty).

¶ Signalling Advantage in Play

When a referee has seen an infringement but is letting play continue under the advantage rule, they signal this to reduce player frustration and prevent retaliation.

Signal (above surface): Index finger extended from a fist, hand moved in a circular motion parallel to the water surface.

This tells players: "I saw it — advantage is being played."

¶ Over-Cautious Behaviour

Do not apply the advantage rule mechanically. Assess each situation:

Is the infringed team actually in a better position continuing than they would be with a restart?

If not, stop play and restart appropriately.

¶ Delay of Game

Any play that is meant to delay or stall, or has the effect of delaying or stalling the game, may be considered foul play.

¶ Corner Rule

¶ What Is the Corner?

The corner is the area bounded by the side and end walls, defined by a 1 m radius arc at each of the four wall intersections. Once the puck touches the arc, the corner rule applies.

¶ The Infringement

Two players from the same team hold the puck in a corner in succession, with neither player:

Making an obvious attempt to move the puck out of the corner, or

Losing possession to an opponent.

¶ Penalties

Infringement
Penalty

1st offence
Team caution + Advantage puck to non-offending team

2nd and subsequent
1-minute time penalty on the second player to hold the puck + Advantage puck

Signal: One hand placed on the opposite shoulder, arm horizontal, elbow pointing at the infringing player.

¶ Flicking the Puck Out of Bounds

A player who flicks the puck out of bounds is penalised for delay of game.

¶ Penalties

Infringement
Penalty

1st offence
Team caution + Advantage puck

2nd and subsequent
1-minute time penalty on the offending player

¶ Exceptions (no penalty)

If the puck was deflected out by an opponent's stick or body after being flicked → no penalty.

If deflected by a teammate's stick or body, no penalty — unless the referee judges it was intentional to delay the game.

If one or more players from the offending team are already serving penalty time, the referee may reasonably assume an out-of-bounds deflection was intentional.

¶ Intentional Teammate Deflection

If the team already has a caution and the deflection was intentional:

The player who deflected the puck gets the 1-minute penalty.

The player who flicked the puck does not receive a penalty.

¶ General Delay of Game

Any other action intended to slow down the game may also be penalised at the referee's discretion, including standing on the pool bottom (Rule 16.3.2) and refusing to restart play promptly.
