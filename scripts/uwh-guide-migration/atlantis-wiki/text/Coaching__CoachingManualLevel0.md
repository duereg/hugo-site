¶ Level 0 Coaching Manual

¶ Scope and Limits

Level 0 introduces players to underwater hockey in a safe, calm, and achievable way. It is the entry point of the Atlantis Sports pathway. This manual defines what is taught, in what order, and to what standard. Detailed drills, diagrams, and videos are hosted on the wiki.

¶ Entry Assumptions / Who Level 0 Is For

New to underwater hockey.

Comfortable swimmers.

No prior underwater hockey experience.

Content applies to juniors and adults (delivery adjusted for age).

¶ Ready to Progress When the Player Can

Enter and exit the water calmly.

Move along the pool bottom with control.

Push and stop the puck while swimming.

Understand basic ideas of attack and defence.

Take part in simple structured play.

¶ Safety Notice (Applies to All Levels)

Players may surface at any time.

Stop if ears hurt or equalisation is difficult.

Report any head impacts before continuing.

Equalise early, gently, and often.

See [/Safety] Safety: Equalisation and Injury Reporting for full safety guidance.

¶ 2. Breathing: Fundamentals of Snorkel Use

Swimming With the Snorkel on the Surface

Swim on the surface, face down, breathing through the snorkel. Keep the head low and relaxed; no need to lift the face to breathe. This enables players to watch the game, remain calm, and move efficiently without stopping.

Clearing the Snorkel

Primary Method: When water enters the snorkel during a dive, clear it by exhaling sharply as you reach the surface.

Head Position and Timing: Tilt your head slightly backward and begin exhaling just before the snorkel tip breaks the surface.

Air Bubble Technique: Create a small air pocket before surfacing to assist clearing.

¶ 3. Movement and Water Entry

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/swimming_at_the_surface] Surface Swimming

Players can swim at the surface by only kicking or by taking front crawl strokes. Ensure the stroke path is clear.

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/kicking_mid-water] Midwater Swimming

 Types of Kicking

Dolphin: Wave motion from chest to feet with engaged core.

Flutter: Kick from the hip, keep knees straight, toes pointed.

Diving

 

 
 [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/diving_while_swimming] Duck Dive: Hard dolphin kick, bend slightly at the hip, dive at an angle.

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/fins-first_dive] Kelp / Fins-First Dive: Angle fins downward, use the free arm to push into the dive, playing hand touches first.

 

Bottom Movement

Body Position

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/cobra_position] The Cobra Position keeps the chest up and hips low, allowing players to stay strong and stable on the bottom while maintaining a clear view.

¶ 4. Basic Stick and Puck Skills

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/puck_handling/standard_grip] Gripping the Stick: Use either hand, but only one hand on the stick at a time.

Swim With the Puck: Keep the puck in front while maintaining control.

Control the Puck (Push Pull): Push forward. Pull back under control.

 [/players/individual_fundamentals/puck_handling/passes] Flick or Passing: Execute short, controlled flicks and passes.

 [/players/evasive_maneuvers/standard_curl] Protecting the Puck (Curling): Wide curling turns while maintaining control.

Simple Skill Combination

Swim then flick.

Push pull then swim.

Curl then swim.

¶ 5. Introductory Roles

Attackers

An attacker is the player with the puck or supporting it toward the goal.

Fundamentals:

Move the puck toward the goal.

Keep the puck safe.

Help the team score.

Defenders

A defender is any player whose team does not have the puck.

Fundamentals:

Get between the puck and the goal.

Slow the attacker down.

Make forward movement difficult.

Roles change constantly; focus is on understanding intent, not fixed position.

¶ 6. Contact in Attack and Defence

Contact Includes:

Stick on puck pressure.

Blocking the puck with the stick.

Pressure while contesting the puck.

Attempting to gain position.

CMAS Aligned — Illegal Actions:

 [/game/fouls/stick_infringement] Stick contacting the opponent’s body. 

Stick contacting the opponent’s stick instead of the puck.

Using the glove or body to hold the puck.

Holding or leaning after the puck moves.

 
 [/game/fouls/obstruction-blocking] Blocking without attempting to play the puck. 

Player Drills:

Attacking and defending space.

Transitioning between the two.

Playing short 1v1, 2v2, or 3v3 games.

¶ 7. Structured Play

Teams

Two teams attempt to move the puck into the opposing goal.

Standard is 6 players per team but can be adapted.

Differentiating Teams

Different colored caps.

Matching stick colors.

Basic Rules

Start with a [/players/individual_fundamentals/moving_in_the_water/strikes] Strike from the wall.

Use the stick only.

No grabbing, pushing, or holding.

Work together to score.

One hand on the stick.

¶ 10. Training Framework

Safety and orientation.

Movement without the puck.

One new skill or concept.

Controlled application.

Calm reset.

¶ Age-Specific Adaptations

Juniors New to the Sport

Focus: Fun before form. Short explanations. Frequent breaks.

Youth Entry Programs

Focus: Intensity control. Managing competitiveness.

Adult Beginners

Focus: Permission to stop. Normalizing mistakes.

¶ Level 0 Exit Standards

Enter water and equalise confidently.

Maintain calm control with the puck.

Understand attack and defence flow.

Play within rules and safety expectations.
