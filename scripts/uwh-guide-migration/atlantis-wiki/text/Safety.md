¶ Atlantis Sports Underwater Hockey

¶ Safety Summary

This document is a short safety reference for players, parents, and coaches. It highlights the most important safety topics for participation in underwater hockey.

¶ Ear Equalisation

What it is

The middle ear is an air filled space. As you go underwater, pressure increases and must be equalised.

What to do

Equalise early, gently, and often.

Equalise on the surface before diving.

Equalisation should feel easy.

When not to dive

Do not dive if equalisation is uncomfortable.

Do not force equalisation.

Important note

When you are unwell, such as with a cold, flu, or blocked sinuses, equalisation can be harder. If you are unwell, do not dive.

Signs of a problem

Pain.

Pressure or fullness in the ear.

Dizziness or nausea.

If these occur, exit the water and seek medical advice.

¶ Concussion Awareness

Head impacts can occur accidentally.

Possible signs of concussion include:

Headache.

Dizziness.

Nausea or vomiting.

Disorientation.

Vision or speech difficulty.

Memory problems.

What to do

Exit the water immediately.

Do not return to play that session.

Seek medical assessment.

¶ Injury Reporting

All injuries and near misses must be taken seriously.

If an injury occurs:

Stop activity if required.

Exit the water calmly.

Inform a coach immediately.

The following information should be recorded:

Date.

What happened.

Who was involved.

Suspected injury.

Reporting injuries helps prevent future incidents.

¶ Key Safety Principle

When unsure, stop.

Stopping early prevents more serious injury.
