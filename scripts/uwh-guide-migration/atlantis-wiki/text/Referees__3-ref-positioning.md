¶ Referee Positioning

Good positioning is the foundation of effective refereeing. A well-positioned referee sees more, decides faster, and earns player trust.

Two guiding principles:

Protect the players (safety first)

Keep the puck playing as much as possible

The diagrams below use a simplified top-down view of the pool. Positions are shown for a 3-Water-Referee system (the preferred setup). In a 2-WR system, WR3 is absent — WR1 and WR2 cover both sides.

 [img:/referees/01_position_game_start.png alt:01_position_game_start.png] 

¶ Start of the Game

Players are lined up along their end walls. The CR gives the 30-second warning, then the start signal.

 [img:../images/01_position_game_start.png alt:] 

What to watch:

All players must have at least one hand visibly touching their end wall.

Substitutes must be in their substitution area.

At the start signal, look for breaking (any player leaving the wall early).

WR1 and WR3 focus on the goal end — players near that goal are the most likely to break.

Signal if breaking occurs: Arm extended straight up, index finger pointing up, hand rotated to inscribe a circle → Advantage puck to the other team.

¶ Puck on the Wall

When the puck is being played along or near a side wall, WRs must reposition to maintain a clear view while staying out of the way.

If the puck goes to the opposite side where the CR is, then the WRs will be naturally positioned with a little adjustment on the distance, no rotation needed.

 [img:/referees/02_position_wall_1-ref.png alt:02_position_wall_1-ref.png] 

If the puck goes to the same side where the CR is, then a quick rotation will be required. WR2 must choose a side to cleary show the other 2 WRs where to position. There is no wrong side for the WR2.

 [img:/referees/03_position_wall_2-refs.png alt:03_position_wall_2-refs.png] 

Key principles:

Never both WRs on the same side without coverage of the other half.

WRs stay close enough to the wall to see puck-to-stick contact, but never block player movement.

What to watch:

Obstruction along the wall is very common here — players using their body to shield the puck.

Free arm violations are frequent in wall play.

Puck going out of bounds — note which player last touched it.

¶ Puck in the Corner

The corner is the most contested and foul-prone area of the pool. The 1 m radius arc defines it — once the puck touches the arc, the corner rule applies.

 [img:/referees/04_position_corner.png alt:04_position_corner.png] 

Key principles:

One WR must get close to the corner — obstruction and delay-of-game fouls here are invisible from distance.

The corner rule activates the moment the puck enters the 1 m arc (not just the corner walls).

Count the possession: two same-team players holding the puck in the corner in succession = delay of game.

What to watch:

Two teammates passing the puck to each other inside the corner without attempting to exit → delay of game.

Obstruction: using the body to shield an opponent from reaching the puck.

The corner rule still applies when attacking — both teams can be penalised.

Corner delay-of-game signal: One hand placed on the opposite shoulder, arm horizontal, elbow pointing at the infringing player.

¶ Advantage Puck

Play has stopped for an infringement. The WRs set up the restart giving one team the advantage.

 [img:/referees/05_position_advantage.png alt:05_position_advantage.png] 

Step-by-step:

WRs align on the puck, then hold up one hand towards the infringing team (stop signal for them).

Both WRs swim 3 m toward the infringing team, forming the imaginary line.

Infringing team retreats behind the line; infringed team can position anywhere.

When ready, both WRs raise one arm (open hand) → CR gives the restart signal.

Infringed team must take possession within 5 seconds.

If the infringing team fails to retreat behind the line before the restart, the puck may be advanced an additional 3 m toward their goal.

Signal: Back up with extended arm (palm toward infringing team) → sweep free arm in 90° arc to indicate the line → raise arm vertically (open hand) when ready.

¶ Penalty Shot

A penalty shot is awarded for an infringement inside the 3 m goal area that prevented a likely goal. The clock is stopped.

 [img:/referees/06_position_penaltyshot.png alt:06_position_penaltyshot.png] 

Setup checklist:

Puck on the penalty spot (3 m from end line, centre of goal)

2 attackers on the surface, behind the puck — selected by the infringed team's captain

1 defender at the end wall, one hand touching the wall — selected by the infringing team's captain

All other players returned to their substitution areas

WRs signal CR (arm raised) when everything is in position

During the shot:

Attacker must take possession within 5 seconds — if not, shot is defended.

Defender keeps hand on wall until the attacker takes possession.

If the puck exits the penalty shot area (fully crosses the 6 m line) → no goal.

If an attacker infringes → no goal, even if already scored.

If the defender infringes → goal awarded.

Signal: Stop play → pump clenched fist up and down vertically.
