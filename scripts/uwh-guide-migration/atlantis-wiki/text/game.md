# The Game

- URL: https://wiki.atlantissports.org/game
- Path: `game`

¶ Description

The official rules of Underwater Hockey (UWH) are available on the website of the sport's international governing cody (CMAS) in two parts, [https://www.cmas.org/document?sessionId=&fileId=5288&language=1] Volume 1 - Playing Area Requirements and [https://www.cmas.org/document?sessionId=&fileId=5287&language=1] Volume 2 - Rules of Play. 

UWH is a [/teams] team sport, with each made up of 6 active [/players] players in the field of play, and up to 4 alternates available “on the fly” as substitute players. The teams are identified by the colors (blue/black vs white) of their one handed [/game/gear-setup/sticks] sticks and head covering ( [/game/gear-setup/caps] caps), and sometimes also by their swimsuits at major competitions. The teams work to push the [/game/gear-setup/pucks] puck into the [/game/gear-setup/goals] goal at each end of the playing area ( [/game/gear-setup/courts] court).

While many teams practice and scrimmage in whichever pool is available to them a regulation [/game/gear-setup/court] court must meet specific length, width, and depth, requirements. Unlike other sports, the total playing area is only part of the equation, UWH has a total playing volume requirement, which is itself a range to allow for differences in pool construction.

A regulation game is two (2) 15 minute halves, with a three (3) minute half-time. Oftentimes smaller tournaments use a shortened version of the time requirements to fit more games in with typically only the finals and medal games being the full regulation time.

The heart of playing UWH is [/players] players are only allowed to interact with the [/game/gear-setup/pucks] puck, and they are only allowed to do so with their [/game/gear-setup/sticks] stick. Most of the player interaction rules stem from this concept. UWH rules have primary goals to ensure player safety and fair play, violating these rules is called a foul and each has specific penalties to be enforced. [/game/fouls] Understanding fouls and the [/game/protocols] protocols that occur after a rule violation is very important for players, coaches, and especially the referees.
