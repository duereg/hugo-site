              Formation Basics

• Our formation will be a 3-3 (three forwards and
  three backs). At times it will resemble a 2-1-2-1, a
  3-2-1 or even a 2-3-1. But everything we do will be
  based off of having three forwards and three backs
  in the water.
Naming of positions is as follows:
• 1 and 2 are the wing forwards. The one closes to
  the wall at any time will be referred to as the
  “Inside” forward and the furthest from the wall will
  be called the “Openside” forward. The only time
  we will talk left and right is for takeoff at the strike.
  During play it’s inside and openside.
• The 3 is the center forward who I will refer to at
  times as the “pivot”
• 4 and 5 are half backs. The one closes to the wall at
  any time will be referred to as the “Inside” back
  and the furthest from the wall will be called the
  “Openside” back. The only time we will talk left
  and right is for takeoff at the strike. During play it’s
  inside and openside.
• 6 is the swing back.
                  Position Overview
• Players 1 and 2 are your outside/wing forwards. It’s important that they
  stay to their side which is left and right based on the location of the 3
  (pivot). The outside forwards are attackers. When they have the puck
  they are able to swim forward and outward at 45degrees or higher.
• The 3 is your center forward/ pivot. In its purest sense, they are to go to
  the puck where ever it is. The pivot’s two main jobs are to either pass
  the puck outward from their location or drop in support for drives with
  the forwards. Either way their actions should keep us on the attack.
  Their first look to pass should be forward and if that is not open they
  should look flat and attempt to hook up with the outside/half backs (4
  and 5) if that is the direction we are looking to move. I would prefer
  they not throw “back pass” and should never go back to the
  swing/fullback (6).
• The 4 and 5 are the halfbacks/wing backs. I view them as inside and
  openside backs. The inside back is the closest to the wall and the
  openside back is the back furthest from the wall. It is rare that the play
  bogs down in the middle of the pool. When that happens the swing
  should move up into the attack and the halfbacks should support. There
  are changes to who is where once we get within scoring range of the
  goal but that’s the basic principles When the puck is in the wall channel
  the inside back should be up close to the play. If the puck comes back to
  them (ie pushed back from the forwards by the defense), they have
  three options of what to do with the puck. They can either pass it back
  up to the forward, swim with it up the wall if the forward surfaces, or
  go 45 forward with a pass to the pivot. If they pass to the pivot they
  need to chase the play until it clears to the other wing and the openside
  back comes up in support. They are not to look to pass to the swing or
  swim lateral off the wall.
• The 6 is the swing/fullback. When the puck is wall side they cover the
  inside back if he is beaten, they are tasked with controlling the middle
  of the formation. If the other team is attacking they need to push the
  puck to the wall side or turn it back to the forwards. If the puck is open
  side, then they cover the outside back and look to push the puck
  towards the wall to stop the attack. This is one of the few positions I’m
  okay with using curls. The swing has the freedom to pass to the
  halfbacks. While this position is primarily a defender in the defensive in,
  they are allowed to attack forward when they see the opportunity
               Style of Play Offense

                                 Vertical
Vertical is exactly what it sounds like. The goal of the team is go straight
down the pool. We aren’t looking to come off the wall or get to the wall.
There are no lateral passes. Everything is pushed forward with the goal of
channeling our attack between the wing forwards. If it goes to the wing
forwards their goal is either to swim straight up the pool until challenged
or to center the puck to the pivot and play containment. Passes from the
outside backs and the swing should go to the inside of the wing forwards
or to the pivot. For lack of a better example, we are going to play the
arrowhead the whole game. No one should look to go lateral and no one
should look to swim outward into open pool. Stay vertical. Defensively, we
work to keep the other team in our channel as the backs look to turn
everything back to the middle of the formation. Backs and wings do
whatever possible not to allow the other team to get outside of our
formation. Normally this is used during a wall game but if we happen to
be in the middle of the pool, the goal is to continue moving up the pool
without our channel of control.

                                   Flow
The main goal of “flow” is to get out in the middle of the pool. Our main
focus defensively is to get off the walls and out into the middle of the
pool. The pivot and the swing should look to always move the puck from
the wall to the middle of the pool. The inside’s (wing and back) should
look to pull it off the wall to the pivot as quickly as possible. Remember
the back should not look to pass to the swing. The swing should look to go
forward to a open side wing or pivot. The pivot should look to go to the
wing or lateral to the openside back. If the other team is looking to bring
it off the wall we should look to force them to go lateral or backwards.
This is our “trap”. We want it off the wall but we do not want to give them
the opportunity to move forward. Once we are off the wall and into the
goal channel, we will look to go forwards. It is okay for the wings to go
outward at 45 degrees or higher, but they are not to go outside of the two
meter line.
              Style of Play Defensively
                                      Wing Forwards
•   Once puck is lost must chase play
•   If puck is on the wall, get between the other team and the wall
•   Force the play to the pivot or swing
•   Don’t allow back passes
•   It’s okay to pick from the outside and curl away from the play to get going on
    offense

                                             Pivot
•   First role is to go to the puck. Attack from next to the other team and allow the
    wings to push the puck to the middle of the formation. Allow the backs to push it
    forward but do not allow the other team to control the middle of our formation
•   If possible take the puck quickly but do not cut off the backs to do this.
•   Do not allow the other team to drop the puck back to their backs or mids (no pass
    backs)
•   Setup in the middle of their formation.

                                       Wing Backs
•   Attack from the outside in unless in front of our goal (can be straight up, the key is
    to not allow the other team to go around the outsides of our formation
•   Funnel their forwards to the pivot
•   Do not let them get outside of our formation
•   Do not let the play go down our wall

                                          Swing
•   Many times the swing is the second defender and the last man back due to the
    fact we have the other back playing so aggressive.
•   Support backs as they push the play inside. When possible, take the puck and go
    on the attack
•   If the wingback cannot get control of the puck and it’s forced to the swing,
    continue to push it across the formation to the other back. Do not give ground
•   Force the other team to go lateral into our players. Do not allow them to go
    vertical.
