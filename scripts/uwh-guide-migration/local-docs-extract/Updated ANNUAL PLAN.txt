General Comments:
	•	Everyone should build his own program.
	•	No one program is better than another one.  Build a program suitable for your needs and goals.
	•	One program for one player. Be aware that using another player’s program is better than nothing, but you may not be at your top level when the tournament arrives if you do not follow your own program requirements. 
	•	A program is something specific.  It should adapt to the player’s physiology, psychology, to the sport, to the player’s life and availability, and to the equipment availability. 
	•	Before building any program, an anaylsis is necessary. Determine the sport requirement, major muscles, type of development you are looking for (strength, speed etc…).
	•	In the following program, Max did an analysis and interpretation of the sport. We might not all have the same vision and not the same analyze. Developing a program is not something universal but individual.
	•	Max is developing five (5) areas under his analysis and is using a specific program for each area. (See below.)
	•	The purpose of sharing this program is to provide keys and tools. Feel free to use it “as is,” but know that it might not be adapted or appropriate for you.  Use your judgment.

Sport Analysis
There are 5 important things to develop in UWH in order to play at high level. 

	•	Upper body strength and power;
	•	Lower body speed, agility, and power;
	•	Core strength;
	•	Breath holding; and,
	•	UWH individual skills, group skills and        strategy. 

1. 	Upper body …Hit the gym…
	•	Strength and power are the most important in order to drive the puck, play the wall, be able to keep the puck, shoot etc… 
	•	Major muscles used are neck upper back, shoulders, forearm muscles, biceps, triceps and pectoralis. 
	•	Do not forget, in gym workouts, you want to develop agonist (the muscle in question) and antagonist (his opposite).
	•	Example - Push / Pull body split
	•	We are using all these muscles as group. We do not use isolated muscles.  We will try to focus our strength and power development on machine free exercises or non-guided machine. Free weight, dumbbell, pulley etc. are all good options. We want to avoid isolation training and we should focus on compound movements. 
	•	Sometimes we will need to use guided machines in order to develop a specific muscle due to injury or weakness.
	•	Muscles strength and power development movements have to be done in the gym as close as possible to the real situation movements. Exercise as if you were in the game.   Examples of upper body exercises at the end of this doc.

2. 	Lower body … jump in the pool…
	•	We are using fins. It is stupid to say but since we are using fins, we need to swim with fins to develop our lower body.  Even runners have to run & ice hockey players have to skate, etc. 
	•	Running and biking are sometimes necessary (and acceptable) because you will not have a pool available.
	•	In order to play well, we need to develop speed, agility, and power. Having strong and heavy legs is pretty useless. We need to be able to start swimming really quickly and get the max speed really quickly as well. We need to swim faster than the others (strike, breakout, swim across the pool etc…). We need to be able to move in any direction, quickly. Really short time reaction is always better and really important for the next level.
	•	Major muscles used: Quadriceps, gluteus, hamstrings.
	•	Everyone is different. Some players are predisposed to be sprinters and others not. The percentage of fast-twitch fibers in your muscles is a genetic factor and there is nothing you can do about it. Maybe you’ll find yourself really fast after completing the annual program and maybe you will just be at your maximum speed but not especially faster than other players. Not a big deal, the most important is to get all the players at their maximum capacity.
3. 	Core … anytime, anywhere…
	•	In order to connect your upper and lower body, you need to build your core.
	•	Developing your core will help you to react and move quickly.
	•	It will help you to connect your upper and lower body while swimming with the puck
	•	It will help prevent back injuries.
	•	Core is used during all movements, all the time.
	•	A routine should be installed. Do not skip a day.
	•	Even if you work out a lot, you might not see your beautiful ABS… not a big deal, doesn’t mean they are not there. Just take a quick look at your food, most people need less than 8% of fat to see them. The goal is to have a strong core even if you don’t look sexy. 
	•	A proper technique is essential. High risk of lower back injuries if core exercises are not done well.


4. 	Breath holding … Dive in the pool…

	•	This part of the development is just as important as the other parts.
	•	Most of players forget about it.  They think that time at the gym, biking, and swimming will save them.
	•	Holding your breath is 80% in your head as it is 80% in your head when you are asked to stay on the bottom while playing in front of a goal until it’s scored.
	•	Pushing yourself during UW workouts will first help your body to adapt, as well as having a better CO2 tolerance, but practicing holding your breath will also develop your anaerobic capacity.
	•	It is important to be relaxed when needed, knowing when and how you can push while underwater, and knowing your limits.  This is why it is critical to build your own program and to become better aware of your capabilities and growth!

5. 	UWH skills & strategy … Sticks up…

	•	Strategy directly depends on the expectations of the coach:  It is based on the coach’s idea of style of play, and he might expect players to be good at some specific skills. A coach who is willing to have his team playing on the wall 100% of the time will ask them to focus on “driving the puck along the wall without gloving“ and “proper strong body positioning on the weak wall.“  Alternately, he could ask players to focus on specific skills to bring the puck to the middle of the pool if he’s willing to propose an open style.
	•	Strategy directly depends on the playbook:  It is based on the playbook, and players will be able to work their positions. It is essential to be at the right spot at the right time.
	•	Strategy directly depends on the position you are playing: Skills may be different if you are playing back, on the left side, or on the right side. If you are going to play on the right side, it is better to focus on skills to bring the puck back to the middle of the pool from right to left…  Think about your position!
Always practice skills and strategy at the maximum speed.

PERIODIZATION

	•	Periodization is the organization of training into workable units.
	•	Units are defined as training sessions:
	•	Micro cycle;
	•	Meso cycle; and,
	•	Macro cycle.
	•	Training session: one workout designed to fulfill a specific purpose. Each training session should have a purpose and goal. The goal can be anywhere from improving your time by 1 second on a sprint, repeated breath holds with little rest, lifting 5 more pounds, or completing an extra rep. 
	•	DO NOT complete a training session without a specific purpose - you are wasting your time.
	•	Micro cycle: recruitment of a number of different training sessions of different types of workouts. Micro cycles can last 7-14 days. Different micro cycles - introduction, restorative (recovery), competitive, and shock.
	•	Meso cycle: made of many micro cycles designed for a specific purpose. Cycles range for 1-4 months. Cycles include introduction, base, strength, power, underwater conditioning, and competitive. Also known as introduction, base, strength, power, and peak. 
	•	Macro cycle: are the 3 big phases in the annual plan:
	•	Preparation phase;
	•	Competitive phase; and,
	•	Transition phase.

General Comments about this Phase

	•	This competitive phase has multiple sub phases. It is however, impossible for us to implement these sub phases in our program. The difference between these sub phases is the time spent practicing the main activity, UWH here. Most of us already practicing UWH as often as possible and we are not able/capable to play our sport once a day (as it is possible for basketball players for example) with coach. But there are few things to focus on during the competitive phase:
	•	In the upper body development, we will be in a phase called “maintenance and plyometric.” The idea is to maintain the strength and power gained in the previous phases. 2 or 3 workouts per week are enough. High intensity but short duration (30-40minutes/workout), focused on few muscle’s group only. Looking at 75-85% of 1RM (see page 48 for loads). Intensity should reach his maximum 2 to 3 weeks prior the tournament. Plyometric workouts will help having quick and powerful movements. This will be necessary at worlds speed.
	•	In the lower body development, this phase is called “specific speed, agility and reaction time“. It means what it means, you should focus on short distances maximum speed, fartlek, stop n go, zig zag etc… as close as possible to the activity, meaning you are going to swim underwater as much as you can.
	•	Breath holding development should be included to the lower body development.
	•	As you understood our body development and breathe holding development are now the same thing. When you go to the pool, you go to swim under.
	•	Play as many games as you can focusing on positioning, strategy etc… Work your skills as much as you can. At this time, we should have a many clinics/training camp as possible in order to help developing the game skills.

NOTE: The red box on the calendar is a phase called “Tapering“. Both volume and intensity are reduced for a week or 2 before the tournament. No more than 2 intense workouts per week. The week prior the tournament, no physical conditioning, sleep and eat well.

How to develop muscles?

	•	It is necessary to determine the 1RM for each movement.

	•	It is important to re-evaluate your 1RM often as you progress.

	•	How many reps, sets, rest? Good question – you’ll find some information below.


1RM

	•	1RM= 1 rep max.  It means: The heavier load you can pull, lift, push, hold in 1 rep only (if you try to do a second movement, it will be impossible).

	•	To progess on a linear progression for multiple months it best to set workouts based on your training max (which is your 1RM).  

	•	Based on this 1RM, you can determine the percentage of load you want to work on.  As you will see below, you are not developing the same thing while working a muscle at 60% or 90%.

	•	It is a really important to remember that muscle development is a science. And as almost any science, numbers are involved. If you do not pay attention to your 1RM, you might spend time at the gym for nothing and/or developing something you are not looking for. Keeping the same load throughout the year is called “standard load.”  Although you will see improvement at the beginning, you’ll reach a plateau and eventually de-train during the competitive phase.

Re-evaluating your 1RM

	•	Your muscles will grow and get stronger... It is actually a really fast process if you are not used to go to the gym.  Re-evaluating your 1RM will help you to stay accurate and to work your muscles at the best you can. 

	•	Based on this annual plan, the first 8 months will be important in terms of progress and improvement. 1RM should be re-evaluated once a month. 
 
	•	After about 4 weeks, it is common to see a “de-load” week.  A de-load week is a week where the exercises will be the same, but you will decrease (not too much) volume and intensity in order to allow your body to regenerate.  You can easily include 1RM re-evaluation during this week.

	•	You do not have to spend 2 hours in a row doing 1RM for each exercise. Do your warm up, pick one exercise, do your 1RM, rest and go for your regular set. 1RM can be easily included to your regular gym workout

How to Organize  Your Week
	•	Check your Availability: As previously said, it is all based on your availability and gym/pool availability. Based on your feeling.
	•	It is however important to remember that your muscles are growing up when you are resting and not when you are actually working out. So organizing your week in order to offer enough recovery time for your muscles and based on the type of workouts you did is necessary.

Example : Week Organization
On a phase 2, mesocycle 2
This is a personal week organization based on personal availability, pool and gym availability, goals etc… at a particular period of an annual plan.
	•	Monday: Aerobic/anaerobic capacity on a bike, interval training. Chest, shoulder and arms for the upper body. UWH at night. 
	•	Tuesday: Aerobic endurance on a bike long distance. Upper body strength workout focused on antagonist muscles (so Back, shoulder and arms different fascicle and muscles). 
	•	Wednesday: Aerobic/anaerobic capacity on a bike. Chest, shoulder and arms upper body workout. UW long distance/time workout. 
	•	Thursday: Upper body workout focused on back, shoulder and arms. Rowing machine, long distance workout. Fin swimming workout, long distance interval training. Puck skill and UWH game. 
	•	Friday: Aerobic/anaerobic capacity on a bike, high intensity interval training. Upper body workout mobilizing all muscular groups, working together. Fin swimming workout, long distance. 
	•	Saturday and Sunday: Full recovery days. If possible active recovery, walking, spearfishing etc…

Recovery Times

	•	It is common to take between 24-48hrs recovery between two workouts (and for most type of workouts) to recover. 
	•	Now, if you are well organized, 48hrs recovery should not mean 48hrs without any workouts. Working one specific muscular group one day and another group the following day is a possible answer. It is the same thing for finswimming and endurance workouts. 
	•	Allowing times between workouts is necessary. It is possible to swim twice a day but one workout will be focused on aerobic endurance and the second will be focused on skill development. 
	•	The most important when we are talking about recovery time is to listen to your body. Your body tells you a lot and it is really important to listen to it.  Many different factors are involved, such as:
	•	Mental recovery, 
	•	Physiological recovery, and 
	•	Neurological recovery  You do not have to be a specialist to understand when you need to take a day off! 
	•	Sleeping well, eating well, drinking (water) well are also really important training factors.
