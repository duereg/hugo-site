Formation Basics Our formation will be a 3-3 (forwards and pivot up front and backs and swing in back). At times it will resemble a 2-1-2-1, a 3-2-1 or even a 2-3-1. But everything we do will be based off of having three up front and three in the back. Naming of positions is as follows: 1 and 2 are the wing forwards. The one closes to the wall at any time will be referred to as the “Inside” forward and the furthest from the wall will be called the “Openside” forward. The only time we will talk left and right is for takeoff at the strike. During play it’s inside and openside. The 3 is the “pivot” and he will anchor the front line and cover the middle of the formation. 4 and 5 are half backs. The one closes to the wall at any time will be referred to as the “Inside” back and the furthest from the wall will be called the “Openside” back. The only time we will talk left and right is for takeoff at the strike. During play it’s inside and openside. 6 is the swing. He will anchor our back line and focus on controlling the back middle of the formation

Flow Style of Play Flow is a style of play that allows us to move from one side of the pool as needed to set up our attack or defend. It is not a freestyle game plan. It requires players to know all player position descriptions and to adhere to the rules of  the  position they are playing. As an offensive style of play, the  objective  is to move the puck from inside to openside  or openside to inside so we can gain pool and set up our attack on the goal. We will attack at angles moving up the pool towards the goal. If the opportunity shows it s elf ,  we will attack vertically and drive to the goal in an arrowhead. That is an option but not our main style. If we are on the wall it is expected that we will look to move up the wall long enough to pull the other team in so that we can go from inside to openside and exploit their over commit. The pivot and the swing should look to always move the puck from the wall to the middle of the pool. The insides (wing and back) should look to pull it off the wall to the pivot when the opportunity presents itself. Moving up the wall is not a wall game but taking pool when possible and looking to suck the other team into over committing to stopping the wall. Remember the back should not look to pass to the swing. The swing should look to go forward to an open side wing or pivot. The pivot should look to go to the wing or lateral to the openside back. It’s important to remember that while this formation is dynamic players must be true to their position description and not “freestyle”. (See position documents for further details). It is okay for the wings to go outward at 45 degrees or higher. Our main focus defensively is to get off the walls and out into the open. If the other team is looking to bring it off the wall we should look to force them to go lateral or backwards. This is our “trap”. We want it off the wall but we do not want to give them the opportunity to move forward. Once we are off the wall and into the goal channel, we will look to go vertical and attack the goal. We cannot allow any team to control the wall.
