Underwater hockey is limited-contact, but diving adds ear pressure, short breath holds, and the odd head knock.

When in doubt, surface.

## Equalizing your ears

Water pressure builds on your middle ear as you go down. Equalize to balance it.

- Equalize **early and often**, not only when it hurts.
- Go gently. A soft pop is enough; do not force it.
- Equalize on the surface before you dive, then again every few feet on the way down.
- Pinch your nose through the mask and blow gently until you feel a release.

**Do not dive through ear pain.** If it will not clear, come up a little and try again. Still stuck? End the session.

A cold, flu, or blocked sinuses makes equalizing harder. Skip deep drops until you are clear.

Pain, fullness that lingers, dizziness, or nausea after a dive: get out and tell a coach or see a doctor.

## Breath holds and surfacing

Game drops are short. You do not need a long hold to play well.

- Surface as soon as you want air.
- Breathe calm and steady before a dive. Frantic panting make the next drop worse.
- Do not hyperventilate before going under.

## Head impacts

Accidental contact happens. Wear a cap with ear protection.

If you experience headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, or vision problems after a knock: get out, skip the rest of the session, and get checked.

## Injuries and near misses

Tell a coach. Early stop beats a worse injury. Near misses matter too; the club can fix hazards before someone gets hurt.
