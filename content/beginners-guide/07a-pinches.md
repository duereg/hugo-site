A **pinch** (also called a **backpick**) is two teammates closing on the player with the puck, or about to get it, taking away their exits, and stealing it. Same play; clubs use either name. [Atlantis — Pinches](https://wiki.atlantissports.org/teams/teamwork/pinches).

Both of you get close, usually one on each side, so the opponent has nowhere useful to go even if they touch the puck first. Stay tight enough that a half-meter escape still dies into a stick or body. Compromise their body position or bottom presence; put the puck where one of you can take it cleanly.

Do not obstruct: camping next to someone without playing the puck can become [screening]({{< ref "01a-fouls.md" >}}); swimming through them is barging. Stick on the puck, bodies legal.

## In the 3-3

When the other team has the puck in your zone, the forward goes down when their back stops the opponent, sandwiches them with the back, and makes sure they have nowhere to go. Rip the puck off the stick, or watch for your back to knock it out and over to you.

Works anywhere on the court, not only in your defensive third. Two on one beats one, almost always, if both stay close.

## Example near the left wall

![Backpick setup: forward closing while the back engages](/beginners-guide/backpick1.png)

A white forward, close by the play, is closing in to pinch.

![Forward low, anticipating the curl](/beginners-guide/backpick2.png)

Keys for the white forward: low to the bottom, anticipate the black player's curl, and be ready to protect the puck once you steal it. As soon as the black player begins their turn, the white forward takes the puck. While stealing, get your body between the black player and the puck so they cannot re-steal.

## Weakside

While the strongside forward pinches with a back, the weakside forward contains and cuts [swings]({{< ref "11a-teamwork-basics.md" >}}): intercept passes into your area, then drive to the wall (safe) or counter if they are out of position. See [Scenario Alignment]({{< ref "08-positioning-by-scenario.md" >}}).
